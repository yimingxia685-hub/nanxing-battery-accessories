01

Buyer intent

Define where the insulation sits before selecting a material.

A battery enclosure sleeve, cell-top ring, terminal barrier, structural plate and high-temperature masking point do not have the same job. Tell the supplier where the part sits, what it touches and how it is assembled before comparing PVC heat shrink, fish paper, polyimide tape or fiberglass insulation sheet.

03

Copy into the RFQ

Twelve fields that make insulation quotations comparable.

  • Cell format, chemistry and pack layout
  • Insulation location and contact surfaces
  • Material or performance requirement
  • Drawing, revision and critical dimensions
  • Thickness and dimensional tolerance
  • Adhesive, liner or supply-format requirement
  • Operating and assembly temperature
  • Humidity, dust, UV or chemical exposure as applicable
  • Sample quantity and approval tests
  • Trial order and forecast volume
  • Packaging, labeling and lot traceability
  • Destination, Incoterm and required delivery window

Ask for technical and compliance evidence tied to the exact quoted material and production lot. Do not treat a general certificate badge as proof for every product or application.

04

Sample approval

Test the material in the intended pack and process.

  1. Freeze the requirement.Record the drawing revision, material or performance target, dimensions, tolerance and supply format.
  2. Inspect representative samples.Check dimensions, cut quality, surface condition, adhesive construction and fit before pack assembly.
  3. Validate the application.Qualified personnel should define electrical, thermal, mechanical, aging and process tests appropriate to the finished product.
  4. Reconfirm the order.Verify the approved sample, current MOQ, price, lead time, document scope, packaging and destination before production.
